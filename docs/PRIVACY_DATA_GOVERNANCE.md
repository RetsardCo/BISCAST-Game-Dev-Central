# Privacy and Data Governance — BISCAST Game Dev Central

## 1. Purpose
Define how the platform collects, uses, displays, stores, retains, and removes personal information and student-created content.

This is an engineering governance document, not legal advice. The institution's Data Protection Officer and authorized officials must review the final policy and applicable obligations.

## 2. Data categories
Potential data:
- Authentication email and provider identifiers.
- Public developer profile fields.
- Academic batch/year information.
- Game and capstone submissions.
- Team credits and awards.
- Voting records.
- Moderation notes and audit logs.
- Uploaded profile and project images.

Collect only what is necessary for the stated platform purpose.

## 3. Public/private classification
| Data | Default classification |
|---|---|
| Published game details | Public |
| Approved developer display name | Public with appropriate consent/policy |
| Institutional email | Private |
| Pending submission | Private to submitter and authorized moderators |
| Internal moderation notes | Restricted |
| Individual voter identity | Restricted |
| Aggregate published results | Public according to event rules |
| Capstone documentation | Public only after approval |
| Audit logs | Restricted |

## 4. Consent and transparency
Before publishing profile information, images, project descriptions, or team credits:
- Explain what will be public.
- Provide a clear consent/authorization process.
- Identify external hosting and embeds.
- Explain how to request correction or removal.
- Do not assume that a student project submitted for a class is automatically approved for public promotion.

## 5. Purpose limitation and minimization
Use data only for authentication, portfolio presentation, moderation, competitions, awards, and approved archival purposes. Do not repurpose student information for unrelated marketing or profiling without an approved basis.

## 6. Retention and deletion
The institution must approve retention periods for:
- Authentication/profile records.
- Rejected submissions.
- Moderation and audit logs.
- Voting records.
- Alumni records.
- Backups.

Support correction, visibility changes, and deletion requests through a documented workflow. Preserve historical credits only where permitted and with an approved policy. Do not invent a fixed retention period.

## 7. Alumni transition
- Verify graduation through an approved institutional process.
- Change account status to alumni.
- Remove student-only privileges by default.
- Preserve approved public portfolio contributions according to consent and policy.
- Provide verified account recovery/contact update if institutional email access ends.
- Do not automatically delete alumni contributions.

## 8. Data subject requests
Create a contact and workflow for access, correction, objection, erasure/blocking, and other applicable rights. Route requests to the institution's designated privacy contact. Record handling without exposing request details publicly.

## 9. Breach handling
Escalate suspected personal-data breaches promptly to the institution's Data Protection Officer. The DPO/legal adviser determines applicability, notification obligations, and deadlines under current law and NPC rules. Do not rely on this document as a legal deadline calculator.

## 10. Governance questions requiring approval
- Who is the personal information controller and who operates the platform?
- What is the lawful basis for each processing purpose?
- Who approves public profiles and capstone content?
- What retention schedule applies?
- Who receives data subject requests?
- Who determines breach notification?
- What third-party processor agreements or institutional approvals are required?
